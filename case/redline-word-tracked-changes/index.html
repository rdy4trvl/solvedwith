LegalWorked

Redline a contract as Word tracked changes

A lawyer had Claude rewrite two sections of a 30-page document and reply to comments as tracked changes.

What they did

Working inside Word, the lawyer asked Claude to revise two sections and answer the reviewer comments, keeping every edit as a native tracked change.

How it went

The lawyer judged the result better than Microsoft's built-in assistant and kept full control of what to accept.

Our read

Our analysis · Oct 28, 2026

Not from the source. Our own assessment of the approach, which may date as the tools change.

Where it falls down

  • One lawyer's judgement on two sections is not evidence that it holds across contract types.
  • A redline shows the changes made; the change that should have been made and was not is invisible.
  • Interactions between clauses across a long agreement are where this is weakest.

Worth trying next

  • Ask separately what it chose not to change, and why.
  • Check the output against the firm's precedent clauses rather than judging the edit on its own.
