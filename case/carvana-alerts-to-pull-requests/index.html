Turn production alerts into investigated pull requests

Carvana's engineering teams let an agent pick up production alerts in Slack, trace the cause, and open a pull request a person then reviews.

What they did

When an alert reaches Slack the agent works out which team owns it and posts a root-cause write-up in that team's format. Engineers can then ask it to open the pull request from its own analysis, which goes to human review before anything reaches production. Each team gets its own scoped identity and its own slice of the data warehouse, tickets, build history and code.

How it went

Alerts in the retail science channel fell 56% once root causes were being fixed, and the wholesale platform team reports answers 65% faster. It replaced two homegrown Slackbots a small team had been maintaining. In one case it walked back its own first conclusion after a retry proved it wrong. The figures are Carvana's own.

Our analysis · Nov 24, 2026

Not from the source. Our own assessment of the approach, which may date as the tools change.

Where it falls down

  • A 56% drop counts alerts, not fixes. Resolving a real cause and quietly silencing a noisy alert move that number the same way.
  • The page records it reversing its own conclusion once a retry proved it wrong, which is the failure mode to expect here: a confident wrong root cause that reads well.
  • The figures are Carvana's own, published by the vendor whose model it runs on.

Worth trying next

  • Track how many of these pull requests are later reverted, not just how many were opened.
  • Keep the per-team scoped access. One agent holding every team's data is a far larger blast radius for the same benefit.
