Cut driver overtime and food costs at a food bank

A 46-staff food bank put an agent on shift patterns, purchasing and reporting. Biweekly overtime fell from 99 hours to 11.

What they did

The team had the agent analyse routing and shift patterns, which led them to stagger start times so overtime stopped falling on the same few drivers. It also compared one partner agency's buying against the at-cost rates the food bank gets from retail partners, parsed exports from an older inventory system to show which mixed loads were worth sorting in house, and rebuilt a monthly reporting spreadsheet. Donor records are deliberately kept out of it.

How it went

Biweekly driver and warehouse overtime fell from 99 hours to 11. The first partner agency to buy through the food bank now spends 40% less on food. A monthly reporting spreadsheet that took a week now takes about 30 seconds. Around 14 of the 46 staff hold seats. The figures are the food bank's own.

Our analysis · Nov 24, 2026

Not from the source. Our own assessment of the approach, which may date as the tools change.

Where it falls down

  • 99 hours to 11 is one organisation's figure over an unstated period, with no before-and-after service level beside it.
  • This is analysis a person then acts on rather than an agent doing the work, so the result depends on the staff member asking the right question.
  • The team keeps donor records out of the tool, which is the right call and also a real limit on what it can help with.

Worth trying next

  • Check the staggered shifts did not simply move the overtime onto a different team.
  • Run the same price comparison for the other partner agencies, since the first one found 40%.
