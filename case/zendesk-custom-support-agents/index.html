Let support teams build their own ticket agents

Zendesk gave its customers a way to describe a support agent in plain English and connect it to their own tools. A million agent runs followed in seven weeks.

What they did

A customer writes instructions in plain English and connects the agent to Zendesk actions and outside tools such as Jira and Google Drive. When a wave of similar tickets arrives, the agent files a Jira issue, posts to Slack so engineers see it, and tells affected customers the problem is being worked on. Once the fix ships it closes the tickets with a note on what went wrong. After each run it reviews its own work and saves what it learned as a skill.

How it went

One million custom agent executions in the first seven weeks of early access, built by a five-person team in four months. Zendesk reports up to 80% lower human handle times and up to 10% more automated resolution for customers running them, and one customer merging duplicate tickets hit 99% accuracy in a pilot. The figures are Zendesk's own.

Our analysis · Nov 24, 2026

Not from the source. Our own assessment of the approach, which may date as the tools change.

Where it falls down

  • The 80% and 10% figures are given as “up to”, which is the best case across customers rather than the typical one.
  • Zendesk is publishing numbers about its own product, carried on its model vendor's site, with no independent check.
  • Customers choose which actions an agent may take, but no approval step on individual actions is described, so a wrongly triaged incident reaches customers before a person sees it.

Worth trying next

  • Start it on internal routing only, and let it message customers once you have seen the triage hold up.
  • Use the per-tool-call log to sample tickets it closed, not only the ones it escalated.
