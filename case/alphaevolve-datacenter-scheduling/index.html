Have an agent write a data centre scheduling heuristic

Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve proposes programs and scores them against automated evaluators. One heuristic it found has run in Google's cluster manager for over a year.

What they did

Gemini models propose programs, automated evaluators verify, run and score each one, and a database of past programs seeds the next round. Pointed at Borg, the cluster manager behind Google's data centres, the loop produced a short scheduling heuristic. The same loop found a better way to split a large matrix multiplication and proposed a Verilog rewrite removing unnecessary bits from a TPU arithmetic circuit.

How it went

The scheduling heuristic had been in production for over a year as of May 2025 and recovers on average 0.7% of Google's worldwide compute. The kernel change sped that kernel up by 23%, cutting Gemini's training time by 1%, and took kernel optimisation from weeks of expert effort to days of automated experiments.

Where it falls down

  • It needs an automated evaluator that can score a candidate objectively. The page says so, and that condition rules out most business problems before you start.
  • Google published this about its own infrastructure, and gives no compute cost, run time or failure rate, so there is no way to weigh 0.7% recovered against what the search cost to run.
  • The figures date from May 2025. “In production for over a year” is relative to then, not to now.

Worth trying next

  • Look for a place you already have a scorer - a benchmark, a cost model, a test suite. That, not the agent, is the hard part.
  • Separate what was deployed from what stayed research: the mathematics results are not described as running anywhere.
